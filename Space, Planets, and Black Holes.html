Space, Planets, and Black Holes

Space

Space is the vast, seemingly infinite expanse that exists beyond the Earth's atmosphere and between celestial bodies. It encompasses everything from the stars and planets to black holes and galaxies.

The Universe

The universe is everything that exists, including all of space, time, matter, and energy. It began with the Big Bang approximately 13.8 billion years ago and has been expanding ever since.

Planets

Planets are large celestial bodies that orbit stars. In our solar system, we have eight recognized planets.

The Solar System

Our solar system consists of the Sun and the objects that orbit it, including eight planets.

Terrestrial Planets

Terrestrial planets are rocky planets, including Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars.
  • Mercury
  • Venus
  • Earth
  • Mars

Gas Giants

Gas giants are Jupiter and Saturn, known for their large sizes and gaseous compositions.
  • Jupiter
  • Saturn

Ice Giants

Ice giants are Uranus and Neptune, characterized by their icy compositions.
  • Uranus
  • Neptune

Black Holes

Black holes are regions of space where gravity is so strong that not even light can escape from them. They are formed when massive stars collapse under their own gravity.

Types of Black Holes

  • Stellar-Mass Black Holes
  • Intermediate-Mass Black Holes
  • Supermassive Black Holes

Specific Black Holes

Sagittarius A*

Sagittarius A* is the supermassive black hole at the center of the Milky Way galaxy.

M87*

M87* is a supermassive black hole located in the center of the Messier 87 galaxy.

Celestial Objects

Celestial objects include a variety of entities found in space.

Stars

Stars are luminous balls of plasma held together by gravity. Examples include the Sun and Sirius.
  • The Sun
  • Sirius

Galaxies

Galaxies are vast systems of stars, star clusters, planetary systems, and interstellar gas and dust bound together by gravity.
  • Milky Way Galaxy
  • Andromeda Galaxy
